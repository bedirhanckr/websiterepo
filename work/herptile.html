Herptile: Exercise System for Pregnancy

A product-service system designed to support safe and controlled exercise during pregnancy, combining land- and water-based training within an integrated experience. ITU Industrial Design, Spring 2022.

ProductStation and reformer for pregnancy pilates

01, Design Brief

Designing exercise around pregnancy

In developed countries, one in seven women experiences postpartum depression. Early diagnosis, regular sleep, nutrition and exercise are known protective factors, yet a safe, supervised exercise environment during pregnancy is out of reach for most women: too little information, too little trust and too few suitable facilities.

Pregnant women look for a safe and accessible way to stay physically active. The key needs: maintain an active and healthy pregnancy, support the health of mother and baby, and improve confidence and sense of self. The barriers: anxiety and fear, transportation, cost and access to appropriate exercise. The design opportunity: how might we make exercise during pregnancy safer, more accessible and easier to integrate into everyday life?

02, System Design

How does the system work?

Connecting healthcare, training, facility and product stakeholders around the user.

The solution couldn't be a single product. Around the pregnant woman we mapped the institutional stakeholders: her doctor, a specialized trainer, family circle, sports clubs, municipalities and the ministries of health and sports. The map clarified how the product had to be positioned within a service system.

The concept was structured as an end-to-end journey: from a doctor's recommendation through profile creation and facility selection to phone-guided reformer sessions and an optional pool session. The journey map tracked the user's emotional state from anxious discovery to satisfied regular use, showing where the service had to build trust.

04, Ideation & Development

Developing the station and reformer

Exploring the interaction, proportions and mechanical relationship between the two product components.

The first sketches set the base dimensions (400 and 450mm) of the reformer bed and the folding leg mechanism. Top, front and side views were developed together in the same sketch set.

05, Use Scenario

Setting up the system

Connecting the reformer to the station and adjusting resistance for land- and water-based exercise:

  1. Connect the reformer's straps to the station.
  2. Reformer and station are connected.
  3. Select the spring stiffness.
  4. Set the spring stiffness for users in water.
  5. The trainer starts the reformer.
  6. Reformer screen: “Hi, please call your trainer to start.”
06, Product Design

The reformer

A compact exercise platform designed for controlled movement and adjustable resistance.

The reformer's core is a sliding platform driven by a cable and spring system. The exploded view shows how the upholstered top plate connects to the lower frame and where the motion is constrained.

07, Product Design

The station

A poolside interface connecting the reformer with the water-based exercise experience.

The station's body was designed to be manufactured by 3mm sheet-metal bending. It was technically defined at 1:5 scale, with orthographic drawings (430, 620, 630 and 340mm).

Sheet-metal bending allowed both the triangular leg structure to be produced from a single bent piece, and the body to fold and be stored easily in shared facilities. This was a decision where material choice was shaped not just by cost, but by service-level requirements like facility space constraints.

08, Technical Definition

Product definition

Integrating the reformer and poolside station into a controlled Pilates experience.

For clarity: no physical prototype was built for this project, validation stayed at the CAD and technical-drawing level. As a result, no real-use or pool testing was carried out either; user validation stayed at the level of ecosystem research and customer journey analysis.
09, Outcome

Designing the physical product together with the service layer

This project was an exercise in designing a system rather than a single object, from a doctor's recommendation to institutional partnerships. Sheet-metal body design and an app-driven user journey sit side by side in one case study, showing product design and service design considered together.

Herptile brings together product and service design to create an integrated exercise experience for pregnant women, supporting controlled Pilates training both on land and in water.
