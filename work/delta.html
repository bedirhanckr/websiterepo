Delta: Convertible Furniture for Space-Constrained Users

Convertible furniture for users with limited space, combining work and play in a single piece. A single telescopic hinge handles the transition between working and reclining positions, no separate parts added.

RoleProduct & mechanism design, individual
ResultDesign for Export competition finalist
Scale1:5 model
MethodCAD, telescopic hinge, 3D-printed model
ToolsBlender
Problem Research Criteria Concept Sketch CAD Engineering DFM/DFA Prototype Testing Iteration Final Outcome
01, Problem

Space-constrained users need furniture that does two things

Users with limited living space need furniture that combines work and play, a desk that also becomes a recliner, or a seat that shifts between focused use and leisure. Most "multi-function" solutions solve this by adding separate parts. The question here was different: how can a single mechanism deliver multiple positions without extra parts?

02, Research

A single-axis sliding joint

Folding and telescopic hinge types were compared. A telescopic joint was identified as the best-suited solution for deriving multiple positions from a single mechanism point, since it allows both angle and length to change along the same axis.

03, Design Criteria

How we decided what was right

CriterionRequirement
Mechanism simplicityPosition transition must be managed from a single joint point
Structural continuityBody material (wood/plywood) must keep its visual integrity across positions
LockingThe mechanism must lock securely in every position
ManufacturabilityMust be verifiable by 3D printing at 1:5 scale
04, Concept Development

One mechanism, two use modes

The concept combined two use modes around the same body for space-constrained users: a working mode with a desk surface for focused tasks, and a reclining/play mode where the backrest angle extends and a footrest appears. The transition between the two is handled by a single telescopic hinge, without adding extra parts to the body.

05, Sketch

Form decisions made directly in CAD

A separate sketch set for this project wasn't archived in a way that could be added to this case study. The mechanism geometry was developed iteratively, directly in CAD.

06, CAD Process

Plywood body, single-piece logic

The body, the sliding wooden leg form and the upholstered seat/backrest surfaces were defined in CAD. The red highlight marks the mechanism's lock points.

07, Engineering Decisions

The telescopic hinge detail

The mechanism's core is a telescopic joint that slides the backrest and seat surfaces along the same axis. The red-tipped lock points show where the mechanism secures in place.

08, DFM / DFA

A single mechanism type, assembly simplicity

The design deliberately stayed committed to a single mechanism type (a telescopic sliding joint). This simplified assembly around one part family, instead of adding separate hinges or lock systems for different positions.

09, Prototyping

A 3D-printed model at 1:5 scale

The mechanism was produced by 3D printing at 1:5 scale and physically validated. The model was photographed with a real dimensional reference (a ruler), so the claimed scale would be clearly stated.

10, Testing
For clarity: this project was developed as a 1:5 scale model. Full-scale load and durability validation wasn't carried out. This is stated openly rather than hidden.
11, Iteration

From a single body to two positions

On the scale model, it was tested whether the mechanism stayed locked in both positions (working and reclining). The location of the lock points was adjusted based on these tests.

12, Final Design

Two positions, one body

13, Outcome

Design for Export finalist

Delta became a finalist and was exhibited at the Design for Export competition, organized by OAİB and the Ministry of Commerce. What makes the project strong isn't furniture aesthetics, it's the engineering decision that solves multiple positions with a single mechanism.
