Rockhounder: Product & Service System for Rockhounding

A backpack and digital service designed to support rock collectors from field exploration to organizing their finds. Graduation project, ITU Industrial Design, Spring 2023: Best Graduation Project Award, 2nd Prize.

RoleProduct & system design, individual
ProductSpecialist bag system (outer bag + inner tool bag) and companion app for rock collecting
ResultBest Graduation Project Award, 2nd Prize (ITU, 2023)
MethodCAD, paper pattern, fabric mock-up, real-use test
ToolsRhino, SolidWorks, V-Ray, Photoshop, Illustrator
Problem Brief Criteria Sketch App Architecture Prototype Field use Definition Outcome
01, Problem

Rock collecting needs specialist equipment, but none exists as a system

Recreational rock collecting (rockhounding) requires specific tools: a hammer for breaking rock, a chisel for digging, protective gear, and a bag that can carry both the tools and the collected rock samples without damage. No ready-made system exists for this. The project was framed as a complete proposal: a specialist bag with an inner tool bag for rock extraction, and a companion app to support the activity.

02, Design Brief

Understanding the rockhounding experience

Defining the user, needs and experience. The persona is an outdoor enthusiast who enjoys exploring new places and experiences, but prefers guided group activities over planning independent trips. The pain points:

  • Dirty and wet specimens are difficult to carry.
  • Field tools are difficult to organize and access.
  • Collected specimens need identification and organization.
  • Standard backpacks are not designed around rockhounding activities.

The concept is a guided weekend experience that combines rockhounding and outdoor activities with equipment and planning support: discover and book, arrive and prepare, explore, rockhound, then create and connect.

03, Design Criteria

How we decided what was right

Referencing the real equipment used for rockhounding and caving (a hammer for breaking rock, a chisel for digging, climbing gear, gloves and a headlamp), we researched how this equipment could be organized inside a single bag.

CriterionRequirement
CompartmentalizationRock samples, climbing gear and electronics/personal items must travel in separate compartments
RigidityThe compartment carrying hard rock samples must protect the other items from damage
AccessibilityEquipment must be quickly reachable on-site without removing the bag
ManufacturabilityThe pattern must be producible with standard tailoring/cutting processes
WashabilityThe stone compartment had to be washable, with a drainage system set into the base of the pack to let excess water out, placed so it does not break the visual whole
04, Ideation & Development

Exploring the field experience

Early concepts explored different ways of carrying equipment, protecting personal gear and supporting outdoor activities.

The bag's internal compartments (rock samples, climbing gear, electronics/personal items) and the grip details of hand tools like the rock hammer and chisel were defined at the sketch stage.

05, App

Extending the experience beyond the field

A digital companion designed to help users explore, learn and connect throughout their rockhounding journey: global chat, missions, stats, settings, search, learning content and a social hub.

The concept was framed as a complete product and app system for rock collecting. The bag system consists of a main carrying bag with a dedicated inner bag that houses the extraction tools (hammer, chisel, gloves). The inner bag can be taken out on-site while the main bag stays at camp. The companion app supports the activity with location, species identification, and route guidance.

06, Product Architecture

A backpack designed for the field

Organizing tools, collected rocks and personal equipment around the needs of field exploration: belt storage for active items, a washable rock storage compartment with water discharge holes, and a toolbox that keeps things organized inside the backpack.

07, Prototyping

From concept to a wearable prototype

Translating the design into a full-scale prototype to evaluate functionality, organization and field use.

  1. Pattern development: flattening the 3D model into 2D patterns.
  2. Paper mock-up: testing proportions and assembly with paper mock-ups.
  3. Sewing and assembly: cutting and sewing the fabric based on the developed patterns.

The bag's 3D surface model was built in Rhino. In the next stage this surface was flattened into a cutting pattern, a step where CAD connects directly to a producible output.

The bag's cutting pattern was first tested on paper, then the same pattern was applied to fabric and sewn by a tailor. This is a real soft-goods pattern-making process, one that also relates to fields like automotive interior/upholstery work.

08, Field Use

Designed around the rockhounding workflow

From carrying equipment to collecting, organizing and cleaning finds in the field: open the main compartment, store the camera body, add a lens, organize and secure, access collected rocks, remove for cleaning.

The mock-up was tested outdoors with a real user: setting the bag down, taking a tool out of it and digging with the rock hammer were observed under real conditions. This stage validated the design through an actual use scenario, not just studio photography.

09, Technical Definition

Product definition

Detailed orthographic views, dimensions and component breakdowns to ensure manufacturability and functionality.

The bag was defined with an assembly logic made of letter-labeled panels (A, B, C, D, E, F and their sub-parts). The cutting line, seam allowance and joining point were specified separately for each panel.

10, Outcome

From graduation project to award-winning design

Rockhounder was developed as my graduation project at Istanbul Technical University, combining field research, product design, prototyping and a supporting digital service. It received the Best Graduation Project Award, 2nd Prize, from a jury that included Otokar and Arçelik representatives. What makes the project strong isn't a single render: it's a verifiable chain from the CAD surface to the paper pattern, from paper to fabric, and through to a real user test.
