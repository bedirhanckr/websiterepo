REX: A Tracked Mini Robot Kit for Robotistan

A tracked mini robot kit with ultrasonic sensors, built on Arduino, designed during my internship at Robotistan. It shipped as a real product with its own assembly guide.

01, Problem

Educational robotics kits struggle at assembly

With Arduino-based educational robotics kits, the real difficulty is usually not the electronics, it's whether the user can assemble the parts in the right order and orientation. The kit is a product that has to be designed as both hardware and an assembly experience.

02, Research

Mecanum wheel and sensor placement

A mecanum wheel system was chosen for mobility. The forward placement of the ultrasonic distance sensors (HC-SR04) and the position of the Arduino Nano shield inside the body were researched with wiring and accessibility in mind.

03, Design Criteria

How we decided what was right

CriterionRequirement
Assembly easeUser must be able to assemble it without electronics experience
Electronics integrationArduino Nano, HC-05 Bluetooth and HC-SR04 sensors must seat cleanly into the body
ManufacturabilityBody must be made from laser-cut acrylic parts
CostMust stay within educational-kit price range
04, Concept Development

A standoff-based layered body

The body was structured as layered acrylic plates connected by brass standoffs. This approach both simplified manufacturing and made it easier for the user to visually track which part goes where during assembly.

05, CAD Process

Early CAD work

The first CAD models were developed quickly in multiple versions to nail down wheel placement and body proportions.

06, Engineering Decisions

Integrating the electronics into the body

The Arduino Nano shield, HC-05 Bluetooth module, two HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensors, geared DC motors and battery holder were defined to sit between layers in a specific order, set by standoff heights (such as 12mm, 45mm).

07, DFM / DFA

The assembly guide is part of the design

The product's most concrete design-for-assembly (DFA) evidence is its step-by-step visual assembly guide. Each step clearly shows which standoff, at which length and in which hole, using labels like A1, A2, Y1-Y4.

08, Iteration

From v2 to the v4.2 final

The project matured not through a single version but through successive fixes: v2, v3, v3.2, v3.3, v3.4, v3.5, v4, v4.1, and finally v4.2. This sequence reflects small but cumulative improvements to wheel assembly and sensor placement.

09, Final Design

Result

10, Outcome

A product that didn't stay a render

REX was sold as a real product under the Robotistan brand, with its own box and assembly guide. This project is a concrete example of why design has to be considered together with production and the user experience, in this case, assembly.
