Urn: A Hot Drink Station for Neighborhood Belonging

Starting from tea's role as a conversation starter in Turkish culture, this system runs on a public transit card and aims to strengthen everyday meeting points in the neighborhood.

RoleProduct & system design, individual
ProductHot drink station running on a public transit card
ResultFull CAD design, production BOM, maintenance scenario
MethodField research, AEIOU analysis, CAD, DFM
ToolsSolidWorks, V-Ray
Problem Research Criteria Concept Sketch CAD Engineering DFM/DFA Iteration Final Outcome
01, Problem

A neighborhood is losing its sense of community

The study started with real field research in Istanbul's Güzeltepe neighborhood (Eyüpsultan): shaped by labor and education migration, with enough green space but no organic meeting point among residents. The question: how do you design a product that makes residents feel more connected to each other and to the neighborhood?

02, Research

User analysis with the AEIOU model

Neighborhood residents were studied in three groups: children, young adults and parents. For each group, activities, objects used, environments and sources of belonging were mapped using the AEIOU framework (Activities, Environment, Interactions, Objects, Users). The analysis surfaced two potential concept directions: "sense of accomplishment" (recycling, feeding animals) and "social interaction" (tea stations).

03, Design Criteria

How we decided what was right

CriterionRequirement
Access modelMust run on a public transit card, without requiring cash or a separate payment system
Outdoor durabilityStainless steel body against weather conditions
HygieneSingle-use paper cup dispensing, contactless use
Maintenance easeTea and cup refilling must be possible without a special tool
Social impactMust encourage social interaction by offering a waiting/seating area
04, Concept Development

The social interaction direction was chosen

Of the two directions, the "social interaction" concept was judged worth developing, because tea is already an existing social ritual in Turkish culture, reinforcing an existing habit rather than teaching a new behavior. The system was designed to sit within the neighborhood's daily route, like a tram or bus stop.

The station was also conceived to collect and recycle used cups in the machine itself, so it had to be easy to maintain and simple to use. As transit-card use grew, promotions such as a free hot drink were meant to encourage public transport and ease Istanbul's traffic by a measure. In the end the aim was to draw people into socializing in their own neighborhood and to make them feel they belong there.

05, Sketch

From a single unit to a small structure

The first sketches explored not a single dispensing unit but a small structure that also housed seating and waiting functions: a tall dispensing tower, a bridge/bench element to sit on, and a game table alongside it. This broad exploration was later simplified around a single unit.

06, CAD Process

Scaled technical definition

The simplified form was technically defined with 1:10 scale orthographic drawings: front, side and top views along with an isometric reference render.

07, Engineering Decisions

Water and power lines defined visibly

Being a hot drink station, the system includes a clean water inlet, a wastewater outlet and a 220V power connection. These three lines were color-coded so they wouldn't be confused during installation and maintenance: clean water blue, wastewater red, power white.

08, DFM / DFA

The maintenance scenario sits at the center of the design

The system's most concrete engineering layer is the maintenance staff's daily refill routine: open the front cover, remove the used tea box, take out used cups, place new ones, refill the box, close the cover. Six steps that make the product's post-production life part of the design, not an afterthought.

09, Iteration

From structure to a single unit

The biggest iteration was scope reduction: the multi-function structure in the first sketches (tower, bench, game table) was narrowed down to a single dispensing unit. This decision increased manufacturability and let the design focus deeply on a single layer, such as the maintenance scenario.

10, Final Design

Result

11, Outcome

A system designed with manufacturing in mind

The project didn't stop at CAD renders. Material and manufacturing method (laser-cut stainless sheet, injection-molded polycarbonate, extrusion) were defined for the main body, covers, drainage trays, tea box and fasteners. A concrete example of a concept turning into a real production list.
